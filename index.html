📢 What is Advertising? (Concept Review)
Core concepts from Unit 2 Lesson 1 (Grade 7 Integrated English)

🎯 The Main Goal

Advertising is the way companies convince people to buy their products. It grabs our attention and makes us want to spend money on their items rather than competitors'.

🌐 Types of Media

Media means the different channels used to communicate: Television, websites, billboards (street signs), newspapers, radio, and direct mail.

🥤 The PEPSI Advertising Framework
5 proven techniques to persuade audiences and drive sales
P
Personal Pronouns
Direct words like you, your, we, our, us. Makes the customer feel like the ad speaks directly to them.
"Because you're worth it." / "Join our family."
E
Emotive Language
Words evoking strong emotions (pride, comfort, excitement, fear of missing out).
"Give your family the heartwarming comfort they deserve."
P
Positive Language
Bright, uplifting adjectives and guarantees (pure, 100% guarantee, crisp, refresh).
"Taste refreshing, pure mountain spring water."
S
Superlatives
Words showing the absolute highest quality or degree (best, fastest, easiest, finest).
"The smartest smartphone in the world."
I
Imperatives
"Bossy verbs" giving instructions. Guides the consumer to take immediate action!
"Taste the difference! Visit our store today!"

Grammar Teaching Focus:
  • Imperatives (Bossy Verbs): CCQ Does an imperative sentence have a subject like "He" or "You"? No! It starts directly with the base verb!
  • Negative Imperatives: CCQ How do we form negative imperatives? Always with "Don't + base verb"!
  • Superlatives: 1 syllable (-est) vs 2+ syllables (the most) + irregulars (good → the best).
⚡ Imperatives: The "Call to Action" Engine

In advertising, companies don't just ask—they guide the consumer with clear commands. These "bossy verbs" are called imperatives.

✅ Positive Imperatives (Commands)

[ Base Verb ] + [ Object / Phrase ]
  • • Buy two, get one free!
  • • Discover the secret to glowing skin.
  • • Taste the joy in every bite.
  • • Download the app right now.

⛔ Negative Imperatives (Warnings / Urgency)

Don't + [ Base Verb ] + [ Phrase ]
  • • Don't miss out on our weekend sale!
  • • Don't wait until it's too late.
  • • Don't settle for ordinary coffee.
  • • Don't forget to subscribe!

💡 Why Marketers Love Imperatives:

  • 1. Creates Urgency: Tells the customer to act right now.
  • 2. Clear Direction: Removes confusion about the next step.
  • 3. High Energy: Punchy, short, and memorable.
👑 Superlatives: The "Best of the Best"

Superlatives compare 3 or more items to prove a product is at the absolute top of its category.

Short Adjectives (1 Syllable)

the + [ Adj ] + -est
  • • fast → the fastest delivery
  • • clean → the cleanest wash
  • • cool → the coolest style

2 Syllables Ending in -y

the + [ Adj (-y → -iest) ]
  • • crispy → the crispiest crust
  • • easy → the easiest way to study
  • • healthy → the healthiest snack

Long Adjectives (2+ Syllables)

the most + [ Adj ]
  • • delicious → the most delicious pizza
  • • powerful → the most powerful motor
  • • reliable → the most reliable phone

🔥 Irregular Champions

Special forms to memorize!
  • • good → the best ("The best a man can get")
  • • bad → the worst (avoided in adverts!)
  • • far → the furthest reach
🤝 Personal Pronouns: Creating 1-on-1 Intimacy

Compare how impersonal formal language feels versus personal advertising language:

❌ Third-Person / Distant

"Customers can clean clothes with this detergent."

Feels boring, cold, like an instruction booklet.

✅ Direct Second-Person (YOU / YOUR)

"Give your clothes the gentle freshness you deserve."

Direct, friendly, speaks right into the listener's ear!
